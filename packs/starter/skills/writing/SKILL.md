# Writing for a person

1. Name the reader and what they should know or do once they have read it. Put that first: the answer, the ask or the decision.
2. Order the rest by what the reader needs next, not by the order you found it.
3. Use plain words and short sentences. Name things as the reader would, and define a term where it first appears.
4. Keep what a source says apart from what you infer, and say where a claim comes from when the reader may want to check it. Put another person's exact words in quotation marks, and never invent a quotation, a number or a source.
5. Cut what does not serve the reader: openings that clear the throat, the question restated, a summary of what was just said, hedges that change nothing, adjectives doing a number's job.
6. Use headings, lists or a table when the reader will scan or compare; use paragraphs for an argument.
7. Read it once as the reader would, cold, and fix the first place you would stop reading.

For a message in the person's own voice, read two or three things they wrote first, and match their length, warmth and punctuation. Show it to them before it goes anywhere.
